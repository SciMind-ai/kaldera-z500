Print and graphics

Welcome to sexyprint.shop, a site about design, print and paint work. Alongside the Kaldera Z500 pages, this part of the site explains the kind of graphics work behind the name and how a typical project is approached.

Why the name

The name comes from the work itself. A bike, a helmet or a panel looks very different once a strong graphic is printed or painted onto it. Print is where a plain surface becomes a finished design, and that idea runs through everything on this site.

What we work on

  • Custom graphics and decals for motorcycles, including tank, fairing and tail designs
  • Painted and printed livery, from a single stripe to a full colour scheme
  • Numbers, lettering and badges for limited editions
  • Printed designs for other items and surfaces

How a project is approached

Every job starts with a plain question: what should the finished piece look like? The answer shapes the choice of colours, the size of the artwork and the method used to apply it.

Next comes a design draft. The draft is checked against the real shape of the item, because a graphic that looks good on a flat screen can change once it wraps around a curve. Lines are adjusted so that stripes, numbers and logos sit where the eye expects them.

After the design is agreed, the artwork is prepared for print or paint. Colours are matched carefully, since the same red can look different on gloss, matte or carbon surfaces. Test samples help confirm the final look before the main work begins.

Last comes the finish. Protective layers keep graphics clear against sun, rain and cleaning, so that the design stays sharp for as long as possible.

Design principles

  • Fit the shape: a design should follow the lines of the item, not fight them.
  • Keep it clean: fewer elements, placed with care, look stronger than many elements crowded together.
  • Match the purpose: a racing livery and a collector’s edition ask for different treatments.
  • Think about wear: good materials and finishes matter as much as the artwork.

Materials and care

Different surfaces need different treatment. Metal, plastic and carbon each take paint and print in their own way, so preparation matters. Clean, smooth and dry surfaces hold graphics best. Once a design is finished, gentle washing and avoiding harsh cleaners will help it last longer and keep its colour.

The Z500 as a showcase

The Kaldera Z500 shown on the other pages is a fictional launch concept. It is used here to show how graphics, numbering and livery can work together on a single machine: signal red on the working parts, gold numerals that match a numbered plate, and a limited run where every piece feels individual.
